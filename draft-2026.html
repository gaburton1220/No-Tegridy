9RBs IN ROUND 2

The Great Running Back Panic

Round 1 went chalk — six wide receivers, six backs, no surprises. Then the room blinked. Nine of the twelve picks in Round 2 were running backs, and by the time the snake turned back around the position was stripped to the studs. Cain Cooksey walked away with Christian McCaffrey and Saquon Barkley on consecutive turns. Everyone else spent the next fourteen rounds explaining why they didn't.

"I had a plan. The plan was RBs. Everyone had that plan." — twelve managers, simultaneously

Naber, What?! Gets His Nabers

Blake Howell has carried the team name for a full year. At pick 32 he finally took Malik Nabers and completed the bit. The league agreed it had to happen. Nobody agreed it was good value.

Free Fall

Kelce and Kittle, Round 7

Two future Hall of Famers went back-to-back at 82 and 83. Barnwell got Kittle, Jacob Johnson got Kelce, and a decade of tight end supremacy officially became a value pick.

A Kicker in Round 8

Brandon Aubrey came off the board at pick 87 — a full 40 picks before the next kicker. The league has a long, documented history with early kickers. It never ends well.

Value

Cooper Kupp at 187

The second-to-last round produced a former triple-crown winner. Cain Cooksey took the flier. Alvin Kamara (141) and Mark Andrews (118) went late too — the veteran discount was in full effect.

Patience

The Quarterback Standoff

Eli Hurt blinked first, taking Josh Allen at 26. Five teams then waited past pick 75, and three held out until Round 8 or later. Patrick Mahomes lasted all the way to 97.

Isaac Got the 1.01

CeeDees Blue Balls is dead. Long live Aaronning Away With It. The league's most reliable punchline drew the top pick, took Gibbs, and followed with Jacobs and A.J. Brown — a genuinely coherent board. Nobody knows how to feel about it.
